# ArWen 2.7.5 development record

The full landing detail behind ArWen 2.7.5, kept verbatim. CHANGELOG.md carries the release note; this page carries the mechanism behind each of its rows.

## 2.7.5 (unreleased)

New:

- `gpuwm cyclone-setup --start-hour N` begins a run at forecast lead N of the selected cycle instead of at that cycle's analysis, so a storm the model only develops at a late lead can be forecast without waiting for the cycle whose analysis contains it. The configuration is initialized from fN and forced from fN onward at the source's own cadence, `start_time` becomes the cycle plus N hours, and `[fetch] forecast_start_hour` carries the same key `gpuwm fetch` already takes. The leads are resolved on the producer's own published ladder, so one it skips is refused by the route that would have to download it. Nothing is source-specific: every registered source that publishes leads takes the flag, a row declaring `max_forecast_hour = 0` refuses it and says why, and a lead past the horizon of the cycle it is asked of is refused naming that horizon, the lead and the lead the window needs. The horizon is the CYCLE's, not the row's ceiling, because several producers run a longer forecast on some cycle hours than others. `--latest-map` takes the same hour, so the centre is clicked on the field the run is initialized from, and the source menu carries each row's declared ceiling. A configuration authored without the flag is byte-identical to one authored with `--start-hour 0`.
- `gpuwm cyclone-setup --nest-budget-gib GIB` sizes the following nest to a memory budget instead of leaving it at the preset's 160x160 whatever the card holds: a 16 GB card was running a nest that fits in under 4 GB because `--card` and `--vram-gib` only priced and admitted the one preset layout. The nest grows up from that preset floor, square, in whole even parent cells, with the parent untouched, and the largest size whose priced tree fits inside the budget wins. It is the same admission the proposal is then judged by, through the same estimator and the same unspent headroom, and the budget narrows the allowance where the card's own free memory enters, so the tile planner's tree road is decided against it too. A budget over what the card has free is bound by the card and the document says which of the two bound it; a budget under the preset nest is refused naming the floor, what it prices on this machine, the number that price was compared against and the way out. That number is the fit target on `--tiles off` and, on the default `--tiles auto`, the smaller admission budget the tree walk weighed the floor against once it withheld the following nest's rebuild transient, quoted with the bytes withheld, the grid they are held for and the walk's own way out; either way it is at or under the price, so the remedy never aims at a number the run already cleared. Each refusal names whichever of the two actually bound it: where the card cannot hold the preset nest the sentence says that instead of blaming a budget larger than the card, reports the card as the bound, and offers as the way out the layout dropping the flag authors, measured on that computer before it is offered. The following tracker's movement bounds are derived from the size rather than carried across from a nest of a different one: an overlap floor f admits a per-axis magnitude of 1 - sqrt(f) of the nest's own width in parent cells on the binding diagonal move, so a grown nest carries the largest maximum its own floor admits on that move and its search box stays half its width on each side. The maximum a size derives is stated as a table on the setup page, because it is read before memory is spent: 6 parent cells at 40 and 42, 7 at 44 through 48, 8 at 50 through 54, 9 at 56 through 60. It is one derivation in both directions, shared with the reduction ladder that proposes nests below the preset floor: the preset's own 6 is what a 40-parent-cell nest's floor admits, so 36 parent cells admit 5 and 24 admit 3, and at the preset's own size the shipped table is emitted verbatim. The chosen size, the parent cells, the floor, the budget, what bound it and the priced memory ride on the document's `nest` block, print as one `plan:` line, and the source menu carries the floor so a form can bound its own field.
- `--source icon-global` runs DWD's deterministic global ICON, a nominal 13 km icosahedral mesh of 2,949,120 cells, from `gpuwm domain`, `gpuwm fetch`, `gpuwm prep` and `gpuwm go` like any other registered source. Aliases `icon`, `icon-13km`, `dwd-icon` and `dwd-icon-global` resolve to it. Eighteen pressure levels from 30 to 1000 hPa, surface and 2 m / 10 m state, the nine-node TERRA soil column with its eight column-mass water layers, sea ice, snow, land fraction and terrain, on a uniform three-hour forcing series to f180 from 00 and 12 UTC and f120 from 06 and 18 UTC. Data source: Deutscher Wetterdienst (opendata.dwd.de), CC BY 4.0.
- A source whose native bytes the mapped engine cannot read may declare an input-normalization stage that runs before the packaged profile is authored. The stage is one more packaged authority, pinned by SHA-256 beside the profile's mapping, composition and provenance documents, carrying the object-name grammar, the cycle grid and cadence, every field's GRIB selector octets and remap method, the level ladders and the intermediate window's envelope; the Python that runs it names no model. Its receipt -- raw input hashes, converter identity, interpolation plan, target geometry and the normalized output inventory -- reaches the prepared tree as a provenance row the composition contract declares, beside the profile's own provenance authority, which travels unchanged.
- `gdt101_remap` joins the bridge bundle: a native remapper for WMO grid-definition template 101, the unstructured mesh whose cell coordinates travel in separate records rather than inside the grid definition. The caller passes the mesh identity, the target window and the seven GRIB selector octets of every record it wants read -- the latitude, longitude and land-fraction records the plan is built from exactly as much as the fields the plan is applied to, because coordinate records are numbered in producer-local code tables -- so it reads any GDT-101 source. It verifies the complete grid identity, cell count, originating centre, product template, reference time and lead of every object before remapping it onto a regional regular latitude/longitude window with a spherical four-neighbour search. A bridge bundle built before this release does not contain it, and preparation names the rebuild instead of falling back.
- Every local DA run carries a nowcast skill number, with no flag. When a window's short forecast completes, the run scores it against the MRMS composite at 15, 30, 45 and 60 minutes after that window's analysis, for whichever of those leads the forecast reached: masked neighbourhood FSS (Roberts and Lean 2008, the observation battery's own engine) on the column maximum of the forecast's stored `REFL_10CM`, at 20, 30 and 40 dBZ in 9 km and 27 km neighbourhoods, with FSS at 30 dBZ in the 9 km box as the primary scalar. The receipt is `arwen.local-da-nowcast-score.v1` at `nowcast-score.json`, beside the window's `complete.json` and named by it, and the compact per-lead form rides on the execution document and on `gpuwm local-da --status`. Until now a local DA receipt counted the observations the analysis accepted and said nothing about the forecast.
- Every model score is published beside the radar-persistence baseline and their difference: the MRMS scan nearest the analysis time is carried forward unchanged to each lead's valid time and scored against that lead's scan under the same thresholds, neighbourhoods and masks, on the identical cells. Inside the first hour persistence is the forecast to beat, so a model FSS with nothing beside it cannot be read. The carried field is an observation, not a model run.
- A lead that cannot be scored is never a zero: a lead whose valid time has not arrived, or whose scan the archive has not published inside the registered publication grace, is `pending` with its reason; a lead whose matching window holds no frame at or above the coverage floor is `missing-obs` with every candidate frame's coverage; a run that could not reach the archive at all is `unavailable` with the error, and the run itself is untouched. A continuous controller scores earlier windows' pending leads as later windows complete.
- Every compact lead row carries `primary_observed_base_rate` and `primary_model_base_rate` beside the model score, the persistence baseline and their difference. A box with no observed echo at the primary threshold scores FSS 1 for the model and 1 for persistence, so the difference is 0.0 and the three numbers read exactly like a forecast that put the storms in the right place; the observed base rate is what separates a clear sky from a skilful one, and it now travels with them onto `execution.json`, `complete.json`, `products.json` and `gpuwm local-da --status` rather than living only in the full receipt.
- `gpuwm local-da --score PLAN` scores every still-unscored lead of a saved case now and rewrites each window receipt, for the leads whose valid time had not arrived when the run finished. MRMS fetching is one library (`gpuwm.obs.mrms_fetch`) that the run, the door and `tools/obs_fetch_mrms.py` all call; it caches decoded packs under `nowcast-observations/` inside the case so a rescore refetches nothing, and it names the archive object it took, its key, its `s3://` URI and its SHA-256, in the receipt.
- `gpuwm enprod` draws ensemble panels into the same folder tree every other forecast product uses, `<out>/<domain>/<product>/<valid-day>/`, on both engines, and writes a `render-summary.json` beside them; the node-side native plot gallery and the five native observation-grid products `tools/da_level2_render.py` draws write one too. A delivered ensemble tree is now indistinguishable in shape and in record from a deterministic render.
- `gpuwm enprod --domain dNN` works on the rust engine instead of being refused as unimplemented, and an ensemble whose members hold more than one nest is refused once, with the same sentence whichever engine was asked for, naming the domains found and `--domain dNN` as the way out.
- `gpuwm enprod --dpi N` means the same picture on both engines: 150 is the engine's own 1200x900 and everything scales from it, so `--dpi 300` renders 2400x1800 instead of being accepted and dropped with a warning. The smallest panel this tree draws is named when a very small `--dpi` reaches it.
- `gpuwm enprod --field` accepts any field the engine's own `--list-fields` carries, and takes each field's title, units, default threshold and unit slug from the engine, so the threshold on a panel means the same thing as the threshold in its filename. A field the matplotlib route has no extractor for is refused by name there, pointing at `--engine rust`; on today's table every name that route accepts has one, so the refusal is a guard on the two vocabularies drifting apart rather than a path a user meets.
- `tools/da_level2_render.py` takes `--engine auto|rust|matplotlib` and draws its five single-panel radar products with the native observation-grid engine, naming the reason in one sentence when it falls back to matplotlib instead. `tools/da_nowcast_render.py` takes the same flag; its sheets are multi-panel compositions it draws itself and it drives no engine, so its deprecated-fallback notice is printed on every run as before and now also names why the single-panel product tier is out of reach when it is.
- The terminal plot catalog (`python -m gpuwm.tui_products --catalog`) states, per preset, every product this lane cannot draw with the recorded reason, and adds the renderer's own verdict for this install when a renderer can be asked. The observation-grid lane's five products are listed as their own lane with what they take. The terminal picker states it too: a preset names how many of its plots this install will not draw and which, and Review carries the recorded reason under each chosen product, with the basis of the statement beside the list.
- `rw_wrfbatch --list-products` carries a stable machine code on every product row beside its prose, lists the ensemble and probabilistic families it used to hide (they still never join `all`), and with no input files also prints what each product needs and what the wrfout import writes, so availability can be answered before a forecast exists.
- `gpuwm local-da --continuous N` runs a bounded continuous regional DA cycle: N analysis windows at the reviewed cadence, each restarting its forecast leg from the previous window's analysis, assimilating the observations of its own analysis time, running the reviewed short forecast and rendering it through the ordinary `gpuwm render` products, in order, with nothing skipped and no scientific setting reduced when a window is late. Every window commits its own immutable decisions under `continuous/window_NNNNNN/` (inputs, analysis, products, completion) and the controller keeps one atomic head, so an interrupted window resumes through `--launch` without recomputing an analysis or re-rendering committed products. `--status PLAN` prints `continuous/status.json`, whose `status` is one of fourteen named values with `controller_alive` decided by the held lock rather than the file's age, and the document a controller leaves behind on exit says it is no longer held; `--stop PLAN` writes a durable stop request the controller honours between operations, or the next launch honours first. A failed window exits with the run refusal `CONTINUOUS_WINDOW_FAILED`, naming the window, the stage and the reason. Proven on node-1 on the 64 x 64 point case with five radars and surface reports: three consecutive windows at 12:15Z, 12:30Z and 12:45Z, 21,611, 23,559 and 26,230 observations accepted, 383 rendered pictures per window.
- The boundary forcing of a continuous case is renewed when a window's short forecast reaches past the forcing the case holds: the same source cycle is prepared again over a longer window through the automatic preparation chain, keeping the initial condition and every forcing frame already run under and appending later frames, and the renewal receipt (`arwen.local-da-forcing-renewal.v1`) records what was kept and what was added. A review bound to a prepared bundle or a local input root is refused by name when a window would need renewal, and frames the source has not published are waited for, for at most one forcing interval past the window's analysis time, before the window fails naming them. The third real window above renewed GFS 12Z from two frames to three and restarted the 12:30Z analysis under the longer inventory.
- A checkpoint can carry the preserved forcing-prefix contract (`preserved-prefix-v1`): `write_restart` and `restore_restart` take `preserved_forcing_prefix`, the header records the immutable setup fingerprint and the forcing interval inventory, and a restart is admitted only when the live inventory keeps every recorded interval byte for byte and appends after them. The sealed extension mode admits a checkpoint only at the exact end of its inventory; a cycling case checkpoints inside an interval, which is what this mode is for. `PreparedRealCase.preserved_forcing_prefix` carries the choice and the ensemble member runner forwards it; an ordinary run keeps the exact-setup rule.
- `run_cycles` takes `first_cycle`, `initial_restarts` and `input_binding`, so one analysis window is one leg with the cumulative clock and a window's cycle manifest records the forcing generation it ran under; a later leg cannot cold-start, and a manifest from another window or forcing generation is refused rather than resumed.
- The rapid-cycling page and the companion protocol describe the nowcast score, the persistence baseline, the pending, missing-obs and unavailable lead states and the `--score` door; `docs/public/CLI-OPTIONS.md` lists `--score PLAN`; `gpuwm local-da --capabilities` publishes the score contract with both schemas, the registered leads, the lead statuses and the `score_command` argument array. The sentence "no forecast-skill claim" is retired from the observation-usage receipt and the pages that carried it: that receipt's counts are still accepted observations and not a skill number, and the skill number is now the nowcast score beside them.
- The companion protocol describes the `continuous_windows` request field, the `continuous` review field, the status document and its vocabulary, the window directory layout, the renewal receipt and the new refusal; the rapid-cycling page gives the commands; `docs/public/CLI-OPTIONS.md` lists `--continuous WINDOWS`, `--status PLAN` and `--stop PLAN`; `gpuwm local-da --capabilities` publishes the continuous contract with the status roster and the argument arrays for status, stop and resume.
- A remote job's review says which route it planned and why: `staged_plan` names the staged bundle a map setup was reviewed from, `node_config` names the configuration file that already exists on the node. Both words ride on the job record and on `gpuwm remote status`, so a reader is told the route rather than left to infer it from which fields were filled in.
- `gpuwm remote status` reports a background map or plot gallery watcher that could not start, as `start_failed` with the reason and the time, and retries that watcher on the next status call, clearing the receipt once it starts. Until now a watcher that failed to start left the job looking as though its maps were simply not ready yet, forever.
- A remote job whose cleanup could not prove its owned processes were gone reports `cleanup_failed` with the surviving process identities, and `gpuwm remote stop` re-runs exactly the same token-bounded signal ladder on exactly those processes. The state stays non-terminal, so watchers do not declare a job with live descendants finished.
- The two remote entry commands come from one declared door table rather than from two literals, so a review and the launch that follows it cannot compose different commands, and an option a door does not carry is refused by name with what that door does carry. The captured configuration or plan is substituted into the command by its declared slot rather than by a fixed argv index.
- `gpuwm remote sync-outputs` retrieves a run's whole committed output set. It walks the run manifest's own commit stream page by page and fetches every committed file through the same verified transfer one frame uses, writing a receipt that names each file with the digest the node committed. A file already held with that digest is not fetched again, so an interrupted retrieval resumes rather than starting over.
- The RUC land surface admits a whole call's inputs with one device read instead of 568. It validated them one array at a time: 122 fields, validated twice over, cost 568 blocking device-to-host reads per land-surface call to decide that nothing was wrong, which is 2,839 of a root step's 3,206 device-to-host reads, 89.8 per cent of them, spent on admission tests that almost always pass. `gpuwm/core/ruc_validation.py` folds the finiteness and range tests for every array in a call into device-side flags resolved with a single read, and the eleven dispatch arms that asked "have I any work?" one mask at a time now decide in one read, converting a mask to column indices once instead of paying a host read on every gather and scatter through it. Measured at 4,096 columns per call: 568 reads per call becomes 17 without snow (12 batch flushes, 5 arm conversions) and 28 with snow, reproduced on an RTX 5070 Ti. The batch resolves inside the call, is on by default, and changes no message: a refusal names the same array with the same wording.
- The batched shortwave chain keeps its chunk workspace on the engine instead of rebuilding it with `cp.zeros` inside the chunk loop. The spcvmc scratch alone is 1.96 GB per 2,048-column chunk at 60 layers, and with the six outputs, the five transposed McICA frames, the band optical depths and the small per-chunk arrays it came to about 2.8 GB of memset per chunk, roughly 50 GB per 36,378-column nest radiation event, 120 GB for an 89,401-column parent event and 11 TB over a five-hour leg. None of it was needed: every one of those arrays is fully written by its kernel before anything reads it, and `SW_SCRATCH_SLOTS` records that slot by slot. The slots now live on the engine as `SWBatchScratch`, allocated once per slot, reused across chunks and calls and released after the radiation event; only the two a kernel reads before writing are zeroed per chunk, `wkl`, which `rsw_inatm_layers_b` scales without having written, and the 4-byte abort flag. Per nest event the memset falls from about 50 GB to about 0.35 GB, more than a hundredfold, and the residue no longer grows with the chunk width. The 2,048-column ceiling on the chunk width goes with it: that number was reasoned against 170 SMs times 1,536 threads, and on a 170 SM part with 2,048 threads per SM the saturating width is 3,328 columns, so the width is now the device's saturation width bounded by the VRAM free when it is first derived, priced by the same function the allocation-accuracy tests hold to the pool.
- The MYNN column width ships at 8,192 columns, the swept optimum, and the card's own derivation rides the receipt one level below it. `mynn_dmp_mf_columns` is 52 per cent of all GPU time in a quiet root step of a 299x299x59 plus 282x129 pair and at the 16,384 columns 2.7.4 shipped it launches 128 blocks of 128 threads, so deriving the width from the card is the obvious move; it was done, measured, and it lost. Swept at nz = 59 through the real forecast door, 200 root steps per arm, two passes with the second in reverse order, sole tenant: the quiet root cycle is 0.5263 s at 16,384 columns, 0.5819 s at 32,768, 0.6170 s at 45,056, 0.6252 s at 65,536 and 0.6395 s at 90,112, monotonic with no knee anywhere in that range, pass-to-pass spread never worse than 0.0017 s, and all ten arms wrote the same frames; the width that card's derivation picks, 65,280 columns, is 18.8 per cent slower per cycle and holds 2.9 GiB more scratch. That sweep started at the shipped width and only ever widened, so a second one ran downward on the same card and the same prepared leg: 0.5039 s at 4,096 columns, 0.4450 s at 8,192, 0.4819 s at 12,288, 0.5268 s at 16,384 and 0.5353 s at 24,576. The optimum is interior, at 8,192 columns: 15.5 per cent cheaper per quiet root cycle than 16,384 on half the workspace, ahead of the runner-up by 0.0369 s against a worst pass-to-pass spread of 0.0037 s anywhere in the sweep, and turning back up by 13.2 per cent at 4,096. The two sweeps overlap at 16,384 and agree there to 0.0005 s, all twenty arms wrote one digest, and the full 1,500-step pair at 8,192 columns with the rest of this release's installed-engine work went from 2,877.5 s to 2,390.5 s, 16.9 per cent, with all 244 frames byte-identical to the 16,384-column baseline. `choose_mynn_column_chunk` is what a run asks for the width: source `pinned-measured` for that default, or `override` for `GPUWM_MYNN_COLUMN_CHUNK`, which is honoured verbatim and refused rather than ignored when it is not a column count. `MYNN_PBL_COLUMN_CHUNK_FLOOR` stays at 16,384 and is now the lower bound of the derivation alone, so a card with no room derives what 2.7.4 ran. The card derivation is kept whole as `would_have_derived`, both ceilings and every device term behind them, inside the receipt's `memory.mynn_column_chunk` block, so no reader can take the receipt for saying the card chose and a sweep on another card has something to argue with. `resolve_mynn_column_chunk` settles the width once per process and memoises it, so the solver, the preflight registry that sizes the shared arena and the receipt all name the same number, and `tools/mynn_chunk_sweep` is the harness that produced the tables; its default ladder now brackets the shipped width instead of starting at it.
- The forecast receipts say whose bytes the card carried. Every GPU-memory view they reported was either in-process, the CuPy pool, or device-wide, because `cudaMemGetInfo` and NVML `memory.used` both answer for the whole card with every process included, so a receipt built from those alone cannot tell a run that grew from a card that filled up underneath it: measured, a 5 h pair of nested runs on a 32 GB card read as "one leg climbs to 29.8 GB and runs 2.3x slower" when that leg's own process sat at 8.2 to 8.6 GB throughout while a second process held 15 to 17.8 GB of the same card and ran kernels on it for 98 per cent of the leg. `gpu_mem_watch` gains three NVML per-process views from `nvidia-smi --query-compute-apps`, this process, every other process on the card, and the card, sampled on their own two-second cadence and never strict: a host without `nvidia-smi` on PATH, or a WDDM driver that reports per-process memory as N/A, records that fact in the receipt and the forecast runs on. A time-above-zero accumulator per probe turns "another process was there" into how many seconds it was there. Both prepared runners spread the split into the receipt's memory section as `this_process_peak_bytes_nvml`, `other_processes_peak_bytes_nvml`, `device_wide_peak_bytes_nvml`, `card_shared_seconds` and `card_shared`, each `None` where NVML cannot attribute memory per process. The watcher's cadence gate and its accumulator also move from `time.monotonic()` to `time.perf_counter()`, because the monotonic clock ticks in 15.6 ms steps on Windows: a 30 ms gap between two per-process readings was booked as 16 ms, and the shared-seconds assertion failed about one run in three there while the same code passed on Linux.

Fixed:
- A following nest holds instead of moving when its search box carries no closed circulation. The 850 hPa threshold is measured from the search box's own minimum, and the existing gate reads only the box that is FLATTER than the threshold; a box under a plain synoptic gradient spans far more, so the cells within the threshold of its minimum are a strip along its own lowest edge and the centroid drawn on them is the box's corner. A cyclone quick forecast started at an open-ocean point with no cyclone near it therefore proposed a jump of 37 parent cells toward a ridge at its first relocation cadence and ended on the overlap floor fifteen minutes in. A vortex is a closed circulation, so a steering surface whose threshold region reaches a box edge the field continues past declines, the hold carries the counts it declined on, and the nest stays where it was placed. The test is narrowed to the cells the answer was drawn from, the qualifying cells within the configured centroid radius of the settled centre, because a real cyclone trails a trough that reaches the box edge without moving the centroid: measured at seven hourly instants of each case with a 5-cell radius, the open-ocean point's settled centre sat 2.41 to 3.13 cells from an open box edge at every instant and a 978.7 hPa cyclone's sat 25.14 to 39.54 cells from one at every instant. A box side that coincides with the plane's own edge is not counted, because `boundary_reason` and a refine grid's `edge_margin_cells` already own that case.
- A scheduled relocation that would breach `min_overlap_fraction` is clamped to the largest move in the same direction that clears it, on the mover and on a containment slide alike, and the nest makes up the rest at the next cadence. The floor was enforced only by a refusal, so a follow source one cell over it ended the run. Every bound that cuts a move is named in the receipt's `clamped_by` beside the requested and executed shifts: `max_move_parent_cells` and `parent_edge` on the mover, and `containment.max_move_parent_cells`, `min_overlap_fraction`, `parent_edge` or `mover_compensated_placement` on a containment slide, whose row also carries the `overlap_fraction` the slide kept and whose requested shift is the slide the deviation asked for rather than the number left after its own cap. `tools/relocation_ledger_audit.py` reads that overlap off every executed move and every slide and checks it against the declared floor, which the run-ending refusal used to make unnecessary; while the containment row carried no such field the check skipped every slide in the ledger and said nothing. The refusal that remains stands only for a caller that names a placement rather than following one, and it states the per-axis bound the floor implies and the knob that would widen it.
- The cyclone follow preset's per-axis movement maximum is derived from its own overlap floor rather than written beside it, and drops from 8 parent cells to 6. Overlap is separable, so a floor `f` admits a per-axis magnitude of only `1 - sqrt(f)` of the nest's width in parent cells on a diagonal move: on the quick-start's 40-parent-cell-wide nest a floor of 0.7 admits 6, and the 8 it declared was a maximum no move could ever use. The bound is a fraction of the NEST's width, so a door that chooses the dimensions derives the maximums after choosing them: `gpuwm.cyclone_setup.follow_table_for_nest` is where the emitted `[domain.follow]` table and the `cyclone.json` receipt both get their two maximums, on the dimensions the proposal settled on, and a fitted proposal shows them among its reviewed changes. Measured through the shipped door on one card at six sizes: the full 160-cell nest keeps 6, a 144-cell nest gets 5, 128 gets 5, 112 and 104 get 4, and a 96-cell nest 24 parent cells wide gets 3. Both cyclone doors write the one table, every shipped configuration and every rung of that ladder is checked against its own implied bound, and each rung of the reduction ladder is priced with the clearance its own nest carries (its search window plus its own maximum move) through that same derivation, which is why the ladder reaches a 0.55 rung: its 88x88 nest, 22 parent cells wide, admits a move of 3 and needs 33 cells of clearance against the exactly 33 its narrow axis has.
- A nest that follows a storm across the antimeridian stops refusing its first move. Source pixels are binned onto the model grid at their canonical column, not the unwrapped index a seam-crossing read carries, so a crop of the sealed statics corridor equals the statics built directly for that footprint byte for byte. A WPS_GEOG index declares a truncated decimal, dx = 0.00833333, whose 43200-fold is 359.999856 degrees, so an unwrapped column names ground 1.44e-4 degrees, about 7.4 m, west of the bytes the read returned: on a 12 km / 3 km Bering Sea tree that moved 198 land-use cells and 40 soil cells between the two builds, flipped the dominant class in five, and refused the relocation at model second 900. The same fold straightens the statics of any single domain whose own source window crosses the seam. Sealed corridors now record the build contract their bytes were produced under, and one sealed before this fix is refused by name with the re-preparation that repairs it, rather than surfacing hours later as a statics mismatch.
- The 4-point and 16-point stencil averages answer for one point the same way whether it is interpolated alone or alongside half a million others. They reduced through a numpy call whose summation order depends on how many points came in the batch, so two builds of the same ground from the same source bytes landed 2 ULP apart in maximum snow albedo, which is enough to refuse a nest relocation on the numpy static route.
- The mapped decode engine's contract marker names the GRIB2 data-representation templates it can read as well as the frameset it writes, so an engine staged before the IEEE-packed (template 5.4) reader is named as a rebuild instead of passing the handshake and then refusing a correct intermediate as "Section 5 simple packing too short" with a re-fetch as the remedy. The two are different contracts and a marker that moved only with the second could not see the first.
- A conformant IEEE-packed GRIB2 message decodes instead of being refused for octets it does not have. Data representation template 5.4 ends at octet 12 and its one template octet is the precision code; the reader routed it to the simple-packing parser, which asks for octet 20, so every such message failed with "Section 5 simple packing too short". The width that parser wanted is what the precision code states, so it is read there: code 1 is 32 bits, 2 is 64, 3 is the 128-bit width the unpacker names as unimplemented, and anything else refuses by number.
- `gpuwm fetch --source era5 --era5-provider arco --retrieve` downloads the archive again. The folder is named once at review, recorded on the job and on its start receipt, and every reader of the job's artifacts resolves it: the manifest binding, the hosted producer chain, the resume prepared-bundle search and the prepared receipt relay. The directory the job was given keeps its own meaning beside it, and the four places a finished run may leave a prepared bundle are spelled once.
- `gpuwm remote resume` takes the same inputs `start` takes, on the node's door as well as the client's. A geography folder, a prepared bundle, a WPS authority, a render selection or a card named on a resume reaches the review, and omitting one still keeps exactly what the source job recorded. On a resumed map plan they are relocated into the plan the run loads rather than passed as flags the run-plan door does not carry, and an option that plan's route does not carry is refused at review naming the route and the options it does carry. A staged map plan's job record names the WPS authority the plan binds on the node rather than a captured file the staged route never writes, so resuming such a job is not refused for a path the operator never gave.
- A map plan that names an existing prepared bundle, WPS authority or checkpoint is relocated onto the node's own copy per key, named by `review-plan --prepared-root/--wps-namelist/--restart`, instead of all three being refused together. The refusal that remains names the flag that relocates it, the manifest's own byte bound and removing the option as the other way out, and the reviewed plan's run options are recorded as the job's own instead of three fixed empty answers the plan disagreed with.
- A remote run may name the card it runs on. `--device` is registered on start, resume and review-plan, carried as the run plan's own device selector grammar, and exported to the forecast child before it can create a context. A selector this node's probe does not list is refused at review naming the cards it does list, and an unmeasured card is priced against the capacity recorded for the card this run selected rather than the smallest of a set it will not run on.
- A remote launch is an attempt the client names, and the node answers a retry of that attempt with the job it already created rather than starting a second forecast on the same card. The attempt identity lives on the job record itself, inside the job folder the node owns, so nothing new enters the durable store. A fresh invocation is a fresh attempt, so running one configuration again is a new run; `--request-id` on start, resume and start-plan names an earlier attempt, the identity rides on every launch reply, and the timeout sentence names it beside the flag that retries it instead of sending the reader to a job list. The deadline for a review, a staged transfer or a launch measures silence rather than duration: the node says it is still working every few seconds and each line resets the client's deadline, so a long review is no longer cut off.
- A node too old for what a 2.7.5 client sends at a slow door is named rather than relayed. The keepalive the client asks for on every start, resume, plan launch and plan review, and the launch attempt it names, are capabilities the node declares (`keepalive_v1`, `launch_attempt_v1`), and a node that refuses either with a bare `unsupported remote request fields` list is answered with one sentence carrying that node's own version, the capability that serves the refused field, the client version to update it to and that the same request can be retried after the update. The staged-plan refusal keeps its named alternative; before this, every launch and plan review from a 2.7.5 client against a 2.7.4 node failed with the field list alone.
- `gpuwm resume` no longer tells an operator that a route cannot write checkpoints. The no-checkpoint refusal appended a route sentence that only a configuration no run can load could reach (an `[experiment]` file with a top-level `domain = []`), and it now states the live remedy on its own: resume needs the `--outdir` of the run being continued, that run must have written a restart (`restart_interval_s`), and resume requires a complete valid set of `gpuwmrst_d*.npz` checkpoints. The remote resume door's refusal drops the same sentence, and the remedy constant that sentence was the last reader of is gone with it.
- A checkpoint written by the resident writer records which memory road wrote it, and both resume doors say so. The restart header carries `written_mode` (the road and the domain shape). `gpuwm resume` prints, beside its continuation line, which memory mode this run resolves `[tiles]` to and, for a file that carries the stamp, which road wrote it, with the contract that makes the pair free (streaming contributes nothing to the restart identity, so a checkpoint written either way resumes either way). The remote resume review carries the same sentences as `resume_notes`, computed through the same door for a discovered latest set and for an explicit `from_checkpoint`, so the terminal's review screen and the job record show them. It is provenance and never identity: the key is excluded from the checkpoint digests exactly as the producer stamp is, so both roads still produce the same checkpoint for the same weather, and a header without the key (a file written before this release, or one the streamed writer produced, which does not stamp yet) says only that the file does not name a road, which is never a refusal.
- The installed cycle bridge worker keeps the interpreter's library directory on `sys.path`. Its bootstrap dropped its own parent directory unconditionally, which costs nothing from a checkout, where that parent holds only the repository, and takes every other installed distribution off the import path when the bridge is installed, where that parent is site-packages: the pinned forecast tree imports cupy at module scope, so the worker died with `No module named 'cupy'` at the first leg of a cycle while every in-tree run stayed green, and numpy, already in `sys.modules` by that line, hid it. The bootstrap now asks the interpreter where installed distributions live, sysconfig's `purelib` and `platlib` and site's `getsitepackages` and `getusersitepackages`, rather than guessing from the path's shape, because a venv, a user install and a system install spell it differently, and scrubs only when the bridge root is not one of those directories, which from a checkout is always. Nothing the scrub was for is lost: the spine guard still refuses `gpuwm.cycle` by name from anywhere, refuses `gpuwm` until it is armed, puts the pinned checkout at `sys.path[0]` when it arms, and the segment receipt still records which tree `gpuwm` came from.
- The manylinux wheel's staged bridge binaries install executable under `pip`. `setup.py` stamped mode `0o755` into each staged member's zip `external_attr`, whose high half is a whole `st_mode` with file-type bits included, and pip's `zip_item_is_executable` requires `S_ISREG`, so pip dropped the execute bits that stamping exists to set and wrote the binaries 0644; the first door to reach a bridge then refused by name with rc 2, naming `chmod +x` as the remedy, while `uv`, which does not apply that predicate, installed the same wheel 0775 and never showed it. Both constants now carry the regular-file type bit. Measured on the rebuilt wheel: all 28 staged artifacts read back `0o100755`, a `pip install` into a clean Python 3.11 venv leaves them 775 with every one passing `os.access(..., os.X_OK)`, and `gpuwm doctor` reports all 28 without a `chmod`; the same wheel re-stamped with the old constant installs all 28 at 0664 and none executable. `tools/build_bridge_bundle.py` already wrote `0o100755 << 16`, which is why the standalone bundles were unaffected and only the wheel carried this. Windows wheels carry no mode bits and were never affected.
- `rw_mpas_convert`'s receipt records the converted frame's own digest under `output_sha256`. It wrote `frame.history_sha256` twice, once under `history_sha256`, where it belongs, and once under `output_sha256`, so a consumer that did the obvious thing and hashed the frame it was handed to compare it with `output_sha256` refused every frame the converter had just written, because the value it compared against was the digest of the MPAS history file the frame came from. `history_sha256` keeps its own value, so a receipt still names both ends of the conversion.
- `state_frame` broadcasts the base pressure on the host, so a state prepared on the CPU writes `PB` like every other field. The diagnostic-pressure branch built it with `cp.broadcast_to`, a device call that refuses the numpy arrays a host-prepared state carries, while `cp.asnumpy` on every other line of the same branch accepts either, so such a state could write every field of a history frame except that one. A device state writes the same bytes it wrote before.
- The package-data coverage check walks `mpas_cycle_bridge/` as well as `gpuwm/`. It asked setuptools which files the declaration selects for `gpuwm/` and the companion distribution only, and `mpas_cycle_bridge/` is the other top-level package the wheel carries and the one whose worker runs from the installed copy and resolves what it reads beside its own modules, so a data file added there and left undeclared would be dropped from the wheel while every in-tree run kept finding it through the checkout. The bridge carries no data file today, so the walk passes over an empty set and trips on the first file no glob covers.
- A real-data initialization is no longer refused because WRF's vertical operator undershot a sharp dry slot. On the `use_sh_qv` lane, specific humidity is carried to the model levels by real's second-order Lagrange operator, which can take a cell below zero where the target level lands inside a mid-tropospheric dry slot, on a forcing column that is finite and non-negative on every source level. That lane is the one lane that never passes through `rh_to_mxrat`, so its interpolated column never met the `qv_min` floor `rh_to_mxrat1` applies to the identical quantity on the relative-humidity lane, and the guard refused the whole domain with `interpolated specific-humidity qv is invalid` on forcing that was valid. The cells the operator takes below zero are now floored at WRF's own `qv_min_value` under WRF's own `qv_min_p_safe` condition, the identical constant under the identical condition, and the floor is receipted on the init result as `prognostic_moisture_floor` with the cells, the columns, the levels, the minimum before the floor and where it was, beside the reference that says what real.exe does instead. Cells at or above zero are untouched, so nothing that initialized before this floor existed moves. The guard is kept for what it refuses: a non-finite value is still refused, and so is a negative at or above `qv_min_p_safe`, where WRF's own rule does not reach, both naming level, row, column and value. The ERA5 and plain-NetCDF ingest lanes share the path, and a child initialized through `nest_init` carries the receipt with the rest of its init result. Measured on the kept column the refusal came from, a 55-level forcing profile through a 43 kPa dry slot: 12 cells in 6 columns on levels 41 and 42, minimum -1.15e-5 kg/kg before the floor at 43,228 Pa.
